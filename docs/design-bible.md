# Supe Pines Design Bible

Supe Pines is a street-level superhero story game about people who keep a
neighbourhood alive when institutions fail them. It is not a power fantasy,
and it should never look like a glossy franchise dashboard.

## The five pillars

1. **Neighbourhood scale.** A block, a bus route, a clinic, a tenant meeting,
   or a corner shop matters more than a skyline-sized threat.
2. **Ink as evidence.** The interface should feel handled: hard brushwork,
   halftone newsprint, imperfect edges, clipped panels, and a Dossier that
   records what the table made together.
3. **Identity under pressure.** A Hero turning from Side I to Side II is a
   story beat. Conditions, names, and the Threat should feel consequential,
   never like character-sheet optimisation.
4. **Warmth inside consequence.** The tone can be funny, tender, or strange
   without becoming weightless. Avoid grimdark cruelty and glossy quips in
   the same measure: keep the people visible.
5. **Shared authorship and safety.** Every surface should make the next action
   clear and leave room for the table to Strike anything without explanation.

## Visual language

- **Palette:** near-black blue/green, dirty cream paper, oxblood danger,
  streetlamp amber, and restrained electric cyan. Cyan is orientation and
  information; oxblood is consequence; amber is the record.
- **Typography:** Bangers is display lettering only. Work Sans carries all
  instructions, controls, labels, and long reading. Never use display type for
  a paragraph or a form label.
- **Surfaces:** cards are hard-edged comic panels with a quiet shadow and a
  visible paper/ink contrast. Do not add glassmorphism, chrome, or decorative
  borders that compete with card content.
- **Motion:** a card may arrive, turn, or settle; nothing should bounce forever.
  Reduced motion removes ambient drift, flicker, deal, and flip transitions.
- **Art:** one readable composition, human scale, no text in generated art,
  no logos, no fake card frames, no grids, and no gore.

## Voice

Use concrete nouns and local details. Prefer “the pharmacy on Ferrous” to
“the city is in danger.” Name what a camera sees before explaining what it
means. The game may be lyrical, but every prompt must leave the storytellers a
door they can open rather than a script they must perform.

## Interaction rules

- A visible control always explains its next action (`Open`, `Turn`, `Trade`,
  `Resolve`, `Return`).
- A card flip is a peek unless the rules explicitly say the card has turned.
- Dynamic counts and waiting states use text as well as colour.
- Focus is visible, keyboard navigation is complete, and overlays return focus
  to the control that opened them.
- Strike, reset, and leave actions are reversible or clearly confirmed.

## Component checklist

Before shipping a new surface, check: readable at 320px; usable with a
keyboard; meaningful with reduced motion; no colour-only status; no raw
`undefined`/`null`; and a clear “what happens next” sentence.
